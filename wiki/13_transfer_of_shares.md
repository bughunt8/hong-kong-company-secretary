# Transfer of Shares in Hong Kong

## Overview
Shares in a Hong Kong company can be transferred from one person (transferor) to another (transferee). Private companies typically restrict share transfers through their Articles of Association, while public companies have fewer restrictions.

## Transfer Restrictions (Private Companies)
- The Articles of Association of a private company usually require:
  - **Board approval** before a transfer can be registered.
  - **Pre-emption rights** – Existing shareholders have the right of first refusal to buy the shares before they can be transferred to a third party.
- These restrictions ensure control over who becomes a shareholder.

## Transfer Process

### Step 1: Execute a Stock Transfer Form (STF)
- The transferor and transferee execute a written instrument of transfer (Stock Transfer Form).
- The STF must state: the company name, number and class of shares being transferred, consideration, names and addresses of transferor and transferee.

### Step 2: Board Approval
- Submit the STF to the company for board approval.
- Directors review and, if satisfied, approve the transfer (subject to Articles).
- Board passes a resolution approving registration of the transfer.

### Step 3: Stamp Duty
- Stamp duty is payable at **0.2% of the higher of consideration or market value** (0.1% paid by each party, or 0.2% total).
- Example: Transfer of shares worth HKD 1,000,000 = HKD 2,000 stamp duty (HKD 1,000 from each party).
- File the STF with the Stamp Duty Office within **30 days** of execution.
- Can be done online via the IRD eTAX portal.

### Step 4: Update Register of Members
- After stamping, the company secretary updates the Register of Members.
- Old share certificates are cancelled.
- New share certificates are issued to the transferee.

### Step 5: File Annual Return Update
- The change in shareholder details will be reflected in the next Annual Return (Form NAR1).

## Stamp Duty Exemptions
- Transfers between associated companies (holding company and subsidiary) may be exempt.
- Gifts to charities may be exempt.
- Apply to Stamp Duty Office for adjudication if exemption is claimed.

## Gift of Shares
- Stamp duty is based on the higher of consideration or market value, so even a gift transfer attracts stamp duty on market value.
- A valuation of the shares may be required.

## Death of a Shareholder
- On death, shares pass to the deceased's estate.
- The executor or administrator obtains a Grant of Probate or Letters of Administration.
- Shares are transmitted (not transferred) to the estate; stamp duty may not apply to the transmission itself.
- If shares are then sold or transferred, stamp duty applies.

## Foreign Currency
- If consideration is in a foreign currency, convert to HKD at the prevailing exchange rate for stamp duty purposes.

## Relevant Legislation
- Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), Part 5
- Stamp Duty Ordinance (Cap. 117)
