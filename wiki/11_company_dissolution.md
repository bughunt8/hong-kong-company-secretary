# Company Dissolution and Winding Up in Hong Kong

## Overview
A Hong Kong company can be dissolved (ceased to exist) through several mechanisms. The most common are voluntary deregistration and winding up (liquidation).

## Methods of Dissolution

### 1. Voluntary Deregistration (Striking Off)
The simplest and cheapest way to dissolve a dormant or solvent company.

**Requirements:**
- Company has not carried on business or been in operation for at least 3 months before application.
- Company has no outstanding liabilities (including tax obligations).
- Company is not a party to any legal proceedings.
- Company's assets do not consist of any immovable property in Hong Kong.
- All members agree to the deregistration.

**Process:**
1. Settle all liabilities and file all outstanding tax returns with IRD.
2. Obtain a "no objection" letter from the Inland Revenue Department (tax clearance).
3. Submit Form NDR1 to Companies Registry with the required fee (HKD 420).
4. Companies Registry publishes notice in the Gazette.
5. If no objection is received within 3 months, company is struck off the register and dissolved.

**Timeline:** Typically 4–6 months from application to dissolution.

### 2. Members' Voluntary Winding Up (MVL)
Used when the company is solvent but shareholders wish to dissolve it.

**Process:**
1. Directors make a Declaration of Solvency (statutory declaration that company can pay debts in full within 12 months).
2. Shareholders pass a Special Resolution to wind up.
3. Liquidator is appointed.
4. Liquidator realises assets, pays liabilities, and distributes remaining assets to shareholders.
5. Liquidator files final accounts and returns with Companies Registry.
6. Company is dissolved 3 months after final filing.

### 3. Creditors' Voluntary Winding Up (CVL)
Used when the company is insolvent.

**Process:**
1. Directors call meetings of shareholders and creditors.
2. Shareholders pass Special Resolution to wind up.
3. Creditors appoint a liquidator (usually an insolvency practitioner).
4. Liquidator realises assets and pays creditors in order of priority.
5. Any surplus is distributed to shareholders (rare if insolvent).
6. Company is dissolved after final filing.

### 4. Compulsory Winding Up (Court-Ordered)
A court order to wind up the company, typically on a petition by creditors, shareholders, or the Official Receiver.

**Common grounds:**
- Company unable to pay its debts.
- Just and equitable ground.
- Oppression of minority shareholders.

**Process:**
1. Petition filed in the High Court.
2. Court appoints Official Receiver as provisional liquidator.
3. Official Receiver or appointed liquidator realises assets.
4. Company is dissolved after the process is complete.

## Restoration After Striking Off
- A company struck off can be restored within 20 years (with court order) or within 20 years (by administrative means if struck off recently).
- Useful if struck off in error or to claim assets discovered after dissolution.

## Post-Dissolution Considerations
- After dissolution, the company's property vests in the Government (bona vacantia).
- Directors and officers retain potential liability for pre-dissolution acts.
- Records should be retained by former directors for at least 7 years.

## Relevant Legislation
- Companies (Winding Up and Miscellaneous Provisions) Ordinance (Cap. 32)
- Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), Part 15 (Deregistration)
- Insolvency Ordinance (Cap. 6) – for personal insolvency related aspects
