# Profits Tax and Tax Obligations for Hong Kong Companies

## Overview
Hong Kong operates a territorial tax system – only profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong are subject to Profits Tax. There is no capital gains tax, withholding tax on dividends, or estate duty.

## Profits Tax Rates
> ⚠️ Tax rates are set by the Inland Revenue Ordinance and may be amended by the annual Budget. Always verify current rates with the IRD website or a qualified tax adviser.

- **Corporations**: 16.5% on assessable profits.
- **Unincorporated businesses**: 15%.
- **Two-tier system** (introduced 2018/19):
  - First HKD 2,000,000 of assessable profits: 8.25% (corporations) or 7.5% (unincorporated).
  - Profits above HKD 2,000,000: standard rate (16.5% or 15%).
  - Only one company per group can benefit from the lower rate.

## Territorial Principle
- Only profits **arising in or derived from Hong Kong** from a **trade, profession, or business** carried on in Hong Kong are taxable.
- Offshore profits are not taxable in Hong Kong.
- Determining whether profits are onshore or offshore is complex and fact-specific.

## Tax Year
- Hong Kong's tax year (year of assessment) runs from 1 April to 31 March.
- Companies may adopt a different financial year-end; the year of assessment is based on the accounting period ending in that tax year.

## Key Tax Forms and Deadlines
- **Profits Tax Return (Form BIR51/BIR52)**: Filed annually; issued by the IRD. New companies typically receive their first return 18 months after incorporation.
- **Extension of time**: Businesses with audited accounts can apply for extensions; tax representatives are granted bulk extensions.

## Allowable Deductions
- Expenses wholly, exclusively, and necessarily incurred in producing chargeable profits.
- Depreciation allowances for capital expenditure (plant and machinery, industrial buildings, commercial buildings).
- Interest expense (subject to conditions).
- Charitable donations (up to 35% of assessable profits, minimum HKD 100).

## Non-Deductible Items
- Capital expenditure (deducted via depreciation allowances instead).
- Private expenses.
- Domestic or personal expenses.

## Salaries Tax
- Employees in Hong Kong are subject to Salaries Tax on employment income arising in Hong Kong.
- Employer must notify IRD when commencing employment (Form IR56E) and cessation (Form IR56F/G).

## No Withholding Tax
- Hong Kong does not levy withholding tax on dividends paid to shareholders.
- No withholding tax on interest or royalties paid to non-residents (with exceptions for certain IP royalties).

## Double Taxation Agreements (DTAs)
- Hong Kong has concluded comprehensive DTAs/CDTAs with many jurisdictions including mainland China, UK, Singapore, Australia, Japan, and others.
- DTAs can reduce withholding taxes on cross-border income.

## Transfer Pricing
- Hong Kong adopted OECD transfer pricing guidelines.
- Transactions between related parties must be at arm's length.
- Transfer pricing documentation required for large groups.

## Annual Filing Obligations
1. File Profits Tax Return with financial statements and tax computations.
2. Renew Business Registration Certificate annually (or every 3 years).
3. File employer's returns for employees (Form IR56B annually, April each year).

## Relevant Legislation
- Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112)
