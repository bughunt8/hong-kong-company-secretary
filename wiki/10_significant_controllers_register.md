# Significant Controllers Register (SCR) in Hong Kong

## Overview
The Significant Controllers Register (SCR) is a mandatory register that Hong Kong companies must maintain, identifying individuals or legal entities with significant control over the company. It was introduced to combat money laundering, terrorist financing, and improve corporate transparency.

## Legal Requirement
- Mandatory since 1 March 2018.
- Required under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), Division 9 of Part 2 (Sections 653A–653Y).
- Applies to all companies incorporated in Hong Kong (except listed companies and certain regulated entities exempt under the legislation).

## What Is a "Significant Controller"?
A significant controller is a registrable person who meets one or more of the following conditions:
1. Directly or indirectly holds more than **25%** of shares in the company.
2. Directly or indirectly holds more than **25%** of voting rights in the company.
3. Holds the right to appoint or remove a **majority of directors**.
4. Has the right to exercise, or actually exercises, **significant influence or control** over the company.
5. Has the right to exercise, or actually exercises, significant influence or control over the activities of a trust or firm which meets any of the above conditions.

## Types of Registrable Persons
- **Registrable Individual** – A natural person who is a significant controller.
- **Registrable Legal Entity** – A legal entity (e.g., a company) listed on a specified stock exchange that is a significant controller.

## Information Required in the SCR
For each significant controller:
- Full name.
- Correspondence address and residential address.
- Identity card or passport number and date of birth.
- Nationality.
- Date of becoming/ceasing to be a registrable person.
- Nature of control (which condition(s) met).

## Maintaining the SCR
- The company must take reasonable steps to identify its significant controllers.
- Must send notices to persons believed to be significant controllers requiring them to confirm or deny their status.
- Must update the SCR within 7 days of becoming aware of any change.
- Must keep the SCR at the registered office or designated representative's address.

## Designated Representative
- Companies must designate at least one representative for SCR purposes.
- The representative must be a director, company secretary, employee, or a professional (e.g., a solicitor or accountant) resident in Hong Kong.
- The representative's details must be recorded in the SCR.

## Access to the SCR
- **Law enforcement access**: Police and specified authorities can inspect the SCR at any time.
- **Not publicly available**: Unlike the Register of Members and Register of Directors, the SCR is not open to public inspection.

## Penalties
- Failure to maintain SCR: company and responsible persons commit an offence – fine up to HKD 25,000 and daily default fine of HKD 700.
- Obstructing law enforcement access to SCR: serious criminal offence.

## Transition to Public Register (2023 Amendments)
- The Companies (Amendment) Ordinance 2023 requires Hong Kong to establish a public beneficial ownership register by end of 2024/2025.
- This will replace the privately-held SCR with a centrally held register at the Companies Registry.
- The transition timeline and implementation details are subject to government announcements.

## Relevant Legislation
- Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), Division 9 of Part 2
- Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615)
